Q2: the honest one is chart ['C'].
    A  bars start at a 29.6 baseline, so only the tips show: a 6.7% rise is drawn as a 500% rise.
    B  a coin icon scaled 1.5x in height AND width: the ink is the area, which grew 2.25x.
    D  bars 8 -> 9 cm for a 100 -> 150 rise: the ink barely moves, the rise is squeezed.
    E  bubble RADIUS set proportional to value: radius x4 means AREA x16.

Q3: ink_dimension = 2 for ['B', 'E']
    Leave it at 1 and you measure the side or radius, not the ink the eye reads as size;
    B and E would both print LF = 1.00 and be passed as honest when they are 2.5x and 5x.

Q4: understated chart(s): ['D']  (LF = 0.25)
    Subject: air-pollution index, up 50%, drawn as up 12.5%. The file does not say WHY the ink
    barely moves (a compressed or offset scale would do it), only that it does. It is still a
    distortion: a reader sees a calm change where the data show a 50% rise, and has no way to
    know. Who benefits is an inference, not something the data say: whoever would rather the
    pollution rise looked small - an agency or an industry, depending on who published it.

Q5: chart F (the dual-axis chart from Part A, case 7)
    revenue line: data +50.0%, ink +1300%, LF 26.0
    cost line:    data +20.5%, ink +225%, LF 11.0
    One chart, two Lie Factors (26.0 and 11.0), because each line has its own axis. The formula
    assumes ONE rule turning data into ink. With twin axes there are two rules, so there is no
    single LF for the chart. And the claim ('cost was higher until May') is about the GAP between
    the lines, which depends only on the two limits the analyst picked. I cannot compute an honest
    number for that, and the reason is the point: the picture is not measuring anything shared.
    Data: revenue above cost in all 6 months; surplus [8, 12, 14, 17, 20, 25] PKR m. Never a loss.
