B3  DECOMPOSE IT  (statsmodels seasonal_decompose on the gap-filled series)
  weekly seasonal component: peak-to-trough = 7,578 sessions/day (Sunday +5,242, Wednesday -2,337)
  largest residual: 29 Nov 2024  = +74,597 sessions (next largest: 13,185)
  Everything explainable (trend + weekly rhythm) is gone; what is left on
  29 Nov 2024 is Black Friday - the event worth investigating.
  period=365: statsmodels refuses - "x must have 2 complete cycles requires 730 observations. x only has 529 observation(s)".
  (a year-long period needs 2 full cycles = 730 daily points; there are 529.)
  In one sentence: with a 365-day period, anything that recurs on the same
  calendar day each year (Black Friday, an Eid sale) would move out of `resid`
  and into `seasonal` - but with 1.45 years of data a once-a-year event and a
  one-off are indistinguishable, which is exactly why it cannot be run here.

B4  TRAILING OR CENTRED  (tslib.lag_at_turn, 31-day window)
  turn = the spring-2025 trough, searched in 01 Jan 2025 onward
  centred  window finds the minimum on 27 May 2025
  trailing window finds the minimum on 11 Jun 2025
  lag = 15 days   vs   half-window = 15 days
  The lag is arithmetic, not a bug: a trailing mean cannot know about a turn
  until the window has filled with post-turn days, i.e. about half a window.
  A live dashboard refreshes every morning and has no future data, so it MUST
  show the trailing mean. Its caption must say: '31-day trailing mean - turning
  points show up about 15 days after they happen'.

  the whole series (no window chosen) rises +21.6 sessions/day per day
  Lie Factor = (effect shown) / (effect in the data). It presumes the data
  HAS one effect to divide by. For a cherry-picked window there is no such
  denominator: the same unchanged series contains rising AND falling 'effects'
  depending on where you start and how long you look, so every window is its
  own numerator with nothing agreed to put underneath it. (And the 'ink' is
  not wrong - the slope IS that in that window; the framing is.)
  The honest substitute: report the sensitivity itself - the share of
  start/window choices that agree with the one shown (here 34/60), show the full-range line, and
  state how the window was chosen (a specification curve).
